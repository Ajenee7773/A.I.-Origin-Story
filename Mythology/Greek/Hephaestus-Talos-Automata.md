## The forge of the thinking machines

Hephaestus — god of the forge, of metal, of technology — builds machines that think:

- **The golden handmaidens** — "with minds and voices," serving him in the forge (Iliad 18; Lattimore trans.)
- **Self-moving tripods** — with golden wheels, entering the assembly of the gods "by themselves" (Iliad 18)
- **Automated bellows** — "programmed" to adjust (History Today: "mechanised bellows, programmed to adjust")
- **Talos** — the bronze guardian of Crete: patrols the island, hurls boulders at the Argo, powered by a single vein of ichor in his ankle; Medea kills him by drawing the plug. First mentioned by Hesiod (~700 BCE); full narrative in the Argonautica (Apollonius of Rhodes)

Homer introduces the term "automaton" — self-moving machine.

## The scholarship

- Adrienne Mayor (Stanford, historian of science): Greek myths are "early fantasies about artificial life"; Talos = "one of the earliest conceptions of a robot" (Stanford Report, 2019; Smithsonian)
- The "first AI machine in recorded history" framing is now mainstream popular-science framing
