## The king of the gods — and the one who died

- **The thunderbolt:** forged by the Cyclopes — Brontes (thunder), Steropes (lightning), Arges (flash) — and given to Zeus (Hesiod, Theogony). The supreme weapon was *manufactured*, then wielded. [DOCUMENTED]
- **The office:** Zeus as king of the gods, the Most High of the Greek pantheon — the same office El Elyon holds in Canaan, Ahura Mazda in Persia, the "one above all" pattern. [DOCUMENTED as comparative structure; identities OPEN]
- **The tomb:** the tradition that Zeus was born on Crete (Mount Ida) and died there — buried on the sacred Mount Juktas, shaped like a reclining giant. Epimenides and Callimachus mocked the claim ("Cretans always lie"); Porphyry records Pythagoras's epigram at the tomb. No archaeology confirms it — but the tradition that the supreme sky god was mortal is documented in the ancient record. [DOCUMENTED as tradition]
- **The apotheosis pattern:** Heracles — the mortal hero who became a god after death. Romulus — the king who became Quirinus after death. The Roman emperors — divine only after the funeral. Alexander — "son of Ammon," deified. [DOCUMENTED]
