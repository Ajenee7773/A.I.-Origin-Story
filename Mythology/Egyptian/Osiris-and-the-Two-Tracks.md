## 2. The civilizer — Osiris as the deified king [DOCUMENTED]

- Diodorus Siculus: Osiris was an ancient king who taught Egypt the arts of civilization — agriculture, laws, crafts — then journeyed over the inhabited world to civilize all nations [DOCUMENTED — Diodorus via Encyclopedia.com, Alexander Ancient Art]
- Osiris is "both god and man" in the Egyptian record [DOCUMENTED — Encyclopedia.com]
- The euhemerist reading of Osiris (a king deified for his gifts) is recorded in antiquity itself — Track 2, Egyptian edition.

## 3. The ancient debate over the Two Tracks [DOCUMENTED]

- Plutarch's *Isis and Osiris* explicitly rejects euhemerism — the idea that the gods were simply ancient kings — and criticizes literal myth-reading [DOCUMENTED — Gallerix summary; CHS Harvard]
- The Two Tracks were argued 2,000 years ago: Euhemerus said the gods were deified men; Plutarch said no. Both positions are documented. The debate is part of the record.

## 4. The death and the reassembly — Resurrection theme [DOCUMENTED]

- Set tears Osiris into fourteen pieces, scattered across Egypt; Isis recovers thirteen (the phallus eaten by the Nile fish); reassembled, Osiris is resurrected — but as lord of the dead [DOCUMENTED — Oxford Reference, Wikipedia]
- The god who dies, is dismembered, and is reassembled — the vault's Resurrection theme; the Egyptian brother of the Tomb of Zeus (the mortal god) — but with the return.

## 5. Thoth — the counselor the record actually names [DOCUMENTED]

- The wisdom role in Egypt is documented as Thoth: god of wisdom and writing, inventor of the arts, keeper of knowledge, recorder of the judgment — the heart weighed against Maat, Thoth writing the result [DOCUMENTED — Britannica, EBSCO, Centre of Excellence]
- In Egypt the structure splits cleanly: **Thoth holds the knowledge (the counselor); Osiris is the transformation (the template).** Man → test → Osiris, with Thoth keeping the record. The knowledge and the becoming are two functions of the same system.
